# Stats we hold or can fetch, and who reads them

- **One source per figure per screen.** Fantrax's counts never sit beside FPL's count of the same thing, and `FPts` only ever means the served league's Fantrax points. Current exceptions: the player's match log (`players/[fantraxId]/MatchLog.tsx`) prints FPL's account left of a rule and Fantrax's right of it, FPL's points headed `FPL`; and the live player card (`components/league/LivePlayerCard.tsx:77–84`) prints Fantrax's category counts in its breakdown (`components/league/Breakdown.tsx:88`) and FPL's goals, assists, penalties, cards and saves under *Full match stats* (`components/league/FplRecords.tsx:71–77`), a heading that does not name FPL. The first is labelled on screen. PLATFORM_NOTES records neither. The pool's `FPts` prices a man at his listed position, not the slot he fills.
- **Reading a table.** One row per stat. *Domain field* names `Type.field` at a repo path and the line where those fields begin. *Read by* is grouped screens · paper · derivations · scripts, with paths relative to `apps/companion/app/` unless they start `core:` (`packages/core/src/`) or `scripts/`. It names the files that read the field's value, each at the line of the read: on a screen, the component that prints it, followed through props (`A.tsx:41 → B.tsx:70`); in the paper, the brief or column builder that puts it in a story (`core:gazette/**`, `scripts/edition/**`); in derivations, code that computes something else from it (attributes, rankings, totals, sort keys); in scripts, `scripts/**`. A file that only fetches, caches or passes the value on is written after `via`, inside the same group. A field nobody reads says `read by nobody` and is listed again under *Fetched but read by nobody*.
- **What the test holds.** `scripts/stats-reference.test.ts` fails when a `Type.field` in a Domain field cell is not declared in that type at the path the cell cites, or the cited line is not the first of the fields (or the line of the function) named before it. It also fails when a field of a type on its `STAT_TYPES` list is written `number`, `number | null`, `boolean` or a `Record` of numbers, is not a key or flag on its `NOT_MEASURES` list, and has no row. The same goes for a field or function on its `NAMED` list with no row, and for a `STAT_TYPES` type that carries no such field at all. It fails when a Domain row outside the stats league cites no field or function of its own that one of those checks requires, so deleting any such row fails; when a table with no Domain field column holds other than the count `PLAIN_ROWS` pins for its section; and when the stats league's rows leave the probe's order or claim a capture. It cannot check a Completeness fraction or a Read-by list: those are records, re-counted by hand.
- **What the tables leave out.** A row per measurement, not per declared field. The test's rule picks out counts, flags and keyed bags of figures. Everything else a stat type declares is left out on purpose unless `NAMED` or a hand-written row names it: keys and ids (`id`, `code`, `playerId`, `teamId`, `gameweek`, `period` and the rest of `NOT_MEASURES`), names and labels, prose (news, commentary, a presser's words), enums such as a fixture's status, and the lists and nested objects that carry another type (`FootballPlayer.season` is a `SeasonTotals`, which has rows of its own). So a field that is a stat in all but its type, such as a form run written as letters, can lack a row without the test noticing. A type off both lists is covered only where a row names it by hand: `RateTotals` (core:football/gameLog.ts) and the `Pedigree` union (core:league/pedigree.ts) are two. FPL's bootstrap sends 75 element keys we never type; *FPL bootstrap keys never typed* lists them by family, so nobody re-probes for season cards or FPL's prices. Its `teams[]` keys beyond `id`, `code`, `name` and `short_name` are untyped too; *Counted and refused* has the ones anybody counted.
- **Completeness** is the fraction somebody counted and the day they counted it; `uncounted` means nobody has. A count quoted from a record keeps that record's date. The record is the one the row names: `.claude/rules/providers.md`, `docs/record/PLATFORM_NOTES.md`, a provider doc beside this one, or the comment at the code, such as `raw.ts`'s. Where the record carries no date, the date is the day the commit wrote it, marked `written`. A count taken for this doc is marked `first-hand`. Those were taken on 25 Sep 2026 from live reads and that day's committed snapshots and intel, and this doc is their only record. One count is marked 26 Sep 2026: taken the day after, with GW5 finished and GW6 not begun, so FPL's answer had not moved.
- **History.** *Stored history and live-only reads* says which of these figures we keep a dated copy of, and which exist only as the provider's answer on the day we ask.
- **Grain**: count, rate, per-90, derived or projection; then match, gameweek, season, career or live. A gameweek figure on a double gameweek covers two matches.
- **Glossary.** *code*: FPL's season-stable player or fixture key, the only one persisted. *elementId*: FPL's per-season player id (`id`), never persisted. *fantraxId*: Fantrax's five-character player id. *club*: a real Premier League side. *team*: a fantasy side in our league. *gameweek*: FPL's round of fixtures. *period*: Fantrax's scoring window, numbered with the gameweeks but on its own dates. *Round*: the gameweek as the app speaks of it. *fixture*: one real match. *matchup*: one pairing of two teams in one period. *fetchedAt*: when a snapshot was read, injected so mappers stay pure.
- The stats league's columns are listed for what they would give us. The squad Stats board reads some of them off that league itself (`statsLeague.ts`), beneath the served league's own.

## Premier League API

`footballapi.pulselive.com`, public, read by `core:football/premierleague/client.ts`: the round read (`/fixtures?gameweekNumbers=…&altIds=true`), the fixture detail (`/fixtures/{id}`), the commentary (`/fixtures/{id}/textstream/EN?pageSize=300`) and the team metrics (`/stats/match/{id}`). Joined to FPL by fixture `code` and Opta player id; `data/mappings/premierleague.json` carries the people. The app caches each read in `plFeed.ts` and asks through `commentary.ts` (the round), `matchFeed.ts` and `matchDetail.ts` (one match), which only fetch and pass on. The paper reads the same endpoints uncached in `scripts/edition/football.ts`.
